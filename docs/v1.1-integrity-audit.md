# MetrEx v1.1 market integrity audit

This audit records pre-upgrade validation. See the [Devnet verification record](v1.1-devnet-verification.md) for the deployed release and live results.

## Contract and operating policy

MetrEx is a permissionless Devnet simulation at admin-registered locations. A market creator controls acceptance for that market. The program does not verify an insurable interest, ownership, or eligibility for insurance. Duplicate markets are allowed and nearby locations can share the same wildfire exposure. Portfolio exposure is gross and grouped by scenario.

The controller closes a market before the oracle can evaluate it. Closure ends underwriting, acceptance, listing, and purchases. Sellers and underwriters can still cancel unfilled listings and open offers. The market locks the accepted coverage at closure, including any partial funding.

On YES, the current Protection holder can claim booked coverage. On NO, the current Risk holder can claim it. The current holder of the other side receives any unsolicited excess. Vault rent goes to the original underwriter. A position trade transfers the claim and refund rights and pays the seller in the same transaction. The escrow balance and booked coverage are independent of the secondary price.

On a void, anyone can distribute the accepted vault. The current Protection holder receives the original premium and the current Risk holder receives the original collateral and any surplus. Prior secondary payments stay with the sellers. A buyer can recover less than their purchase price. The program closes the vault on distribution and makes the offer terminal. Unaccepted offers remain recoverable by their originating underwriters.

The admin can void unfunded markets. Funded recovery requires a Closed market and two distinct signers, the admin and the configured controller. The controller can close Open markets at any time for the replay. The admin and controller can jointly choose to void a funded market before resolution. The oracle can choose the reported outcome on a Closed market and commits the evidence hash. The upgrade authority can change the program. Separation of signers and honest operation are essential trust assumptions. No role receives authority to choose arbitrary refund recipients under this build.

## Findings and dispositions

| Area | Disposition |
|---|---|
| Public perimeter, property outcomes and static build output | Confirmed and fixed in the app. Builds remove the old static copy and omit private settlement data from the trace. Trading property records contain coordinates and terms. Source history, archival documents, existing settled Devnet accounts and public historical records can still reveal the same event. |
| Raw and JSON evidence, failed or delayed resolution | Confirmed and fixed. The server checks the market's program owner and canonical PDA at finalized commitment, then requires the matching resolved side and evidence hash. Raw evidence and the perimeter use the same gate. The resolver checks finalized closure before evaluation, waits for finalized settlement, writes the evidence afterward, and rechecks chain state after ambiguous sends. A retry can recreate missing files against the committed hash. Public settlement errors use a generic response. |
| Open versus Closed trading | Confirmed and fixed. Listings and buys now require Open on chain. Placement and acceptance already required Open. Conflicting transactions serialize through account locks and revalidate current state. |
| Funded void and transferred refunds | Confirmed and fixed. Former holders cannot receive accepted refunds by supplying their token accounts. Funded emergency recovery uses joint authorization. Unfunded voids remain available to the admin. |
| Unsupported scenarios and capacity | Confirmed and fixed. Scenario 1 is the supported on-chain scenario. Registration, creation, and new funding reject unsupported IDs. Offers cannot exceed currently remaining coverage. Competing offers may individually fit while their aggregate exceeds capacity. If an acceptance makes another offer too large, the underwriter can cancel it and the bot does so automatically. A remainder below the 10 mUSD minimum can settle partially funded. |
| Price and transfer integrity | Existing contract already transfers payment and the claim right atomically, checks the seller against the current holder, forbids self/opposite-side trades, and closes a purchased listing. The existing app quote guard protects an exact price even when the listing PDA is reused. Custom direct clients should bind the quoted price too, using the same exact-payment account pattern. Added races and cutoff tests. The market shows the quote's maximum loss, conditional payout, and break-even winning probability before fees. |
| Premium, collateral, payout and surplus | The existing integer formula and checked arithmetic are correct at the tested bounds. Third-party claims had a surplus-recipient defect. The program and wallet now use the current holder of the contractual losing side. The program pays exactly the booked coverage to the winner. |
| Bot exposure and quoting | Confirmed and fixed. A sold Risk position leaves held exposure, while a purchased one enters it. Open-offer collateral and held accepted Risk are counted separately. The cap uses gross offered and held coverage across markets. Bots quote against the remaining capacity, reclaim stale quotes and listings, and use fixed illustrative prices and deterministic rules. The demo fleet ignores sibling quotes to preserve stable sample pricing and competes against outside wallets. Markets can settle with partial coverage if an underwriter is offline or short of capital. |
| Portfolio | Confirmed and fixed. Escrowed premiums are recorded as original gross premiums booked and paid. They are not treated as cash earned or netted against refunds. Current holder, open-offer collateral, active exposure, and claimable booked coverage come from account state. Realized and unrealized P&L and secondary basis are omitted because the wallet's recent RPC activity window cannot reconstruct all purchases, sales, claim proceeds, refunds, and fees. |
| Claims, racing submissions and partial batches | Existing paid-out offer and listing state prevents duplicate terminal transfers. Coverage and wallet balances are checked again on chain. Wallet rejection can leave preceding batch transactions confirmed. The UI preserves those signatures and refreshes chain state. Random u64 market nonces remove the timestamp collision mode. A user who loses network connectivity after sending should reconcile the transaction before creating a replacement. |
| Missing accounts and unattended claims | The app creates missing associated token accounts in the transaction and provides valid destinations for voids and claim surplus. Claims have no expiry or administrative sweep. An absent winner's funds stay in escrow until that holder signs a claim. |
| Datasets and numerical boundary | The oracle reads coordinates and threshold from the Market, verifies the configured normalized file's hash and checks canonical evidence before submitting. Missing, malformed or mismatched data prevent publication. Trigger comparison includes exactly 500 m and uses unrounded distance. |
| Faucet and operator controls | Keeps the demo faucet working with a shared per-process minute limit and the existing wallet cooldown and cap. An ambiguous faucet submission retains its wallet reservation. Operator credentials stay in component memory and share a per-process failed-attempt limit across close and resolve. These limits reset on restart, do not identify people, and can be exhausted by a caller on a public deployment. |
| Polling, empty states and transaction feedback | Existing empty states and error feedback remain. Trade eligibility follows refreshed market state, and already confirmed batch signatures remain visible when a later part fails. Evidence and perimeter rendering check the selected market and retry publication when finalized state or files are still catching up. |

## Historical information boundary

The trading runtime receives no unresolved market evidence or final geometry from MetrEx. The production app needs a separate `METREX_ORACLE_DATA_DIR` mount containing the registry and normalized dataset, plus writable, persistent `evidence/` storage. Restrict the mount to the settlement process and run bots with their own funding keys and filesystem permissions. A development checkout can load the bundled archived dataset for local tests. Remove any previously deployed public perimeter and invalidate cached copies if applicable.

The first publication of a perimeter can reveal results for other markets on the same scenario, including duplicates and subsequent replays. An earlier recorded settlement can do the same. Devnet and public fire records already contain these inputs. The app does not impose a scenario-wide trading embargo because the same event is deliberately replayable. Close the markets in a coordinated demonstration before publishing their shared outcome. This design does not provide cryptographic secrecy or real pre-event price discovery.

## Validation

- **Local Anchor/SBF suite, 78 passed.** Added tests for unsupported scenarios, partial and full funding, accept-versus-cancel contention, competing buyers, secondary cutoff, two transfers per side followed by a void, unauthorized cancellation and refunds, transferred-claim surplus, and a real finalized oracle-to-claim path. Prior tests still cover duplicate claims, mint and owner checks, stale listings, exact-price rollback and conservation.
- **Workspace TypeScript tests, 124 passed.** Added regression coverage for program ownership and PDA verification, all evidence formats before settlement, real perimeter publication and tampered datasets, failed settlement followed by a confirmed retry, shared request limits, exact 500 m comparison, bought and sold Risk exposure, portfolio holdings after resales and repurchase, and partial wallet batch recovery.
- **Build, lint and type checks passed.** `anchor build`, `cargo test --workspace`, `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets`, `pnpm lint:ts`, `pnpm exec tsc -p tsconfig.json --noEmit`, and `pnpm --filter app build`. The generated IDL matches the prior IDL apart from the added error. The tested SBF artifact is 563,936 bytes and its SHA-256 is `e717b779666a94ac866d4d636bb4846229010f24b9c7aba239117039b4c91348`.
- **Production HTTP smoke passed.** The former static perimeter URL returned 404. JSON evidence, raw evidence, and perimeter requests for an open Devnet market returned 404. A previously settled Devnet market served byte-identical archived evidence and a valid MultiPolygon. The production client chunks and deployment traces contained no copy of the perimeter or private dataset.
- **Local application lifecycle passed.** Manual YES, bot-funded YES and NO, and Tier 2 Risk purchase followed by NO payout all used real local-validator transactions. Each run distributed exactly 10,000 mUSD. Tier 2 also exercised the faucet, external undercut and bot requotes, and the stats route. The initial combined run used minimum faucet funding for two grants and ran short before Tier 2. The successful Tier 2 run used a fresh local validator. These runs used app-client signers, so the revised build still needs a live Phantom rehearsal after the authorized Devnet upgrade.

The build is ready to stage for an authorized Devnet upgrade and then an end-to-end Devnet rehearsal. The replay remains a public-history simulation with cross-market inference risk.
